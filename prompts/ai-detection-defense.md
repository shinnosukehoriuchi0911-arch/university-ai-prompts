# AI Detection Defense & Academic Integrity Prompt

Use this prompt to keep a transparent record of how you used AI as a thinking partner, so you can explain your process if an AI detector (Turnitin, GPTZero) wrongly flags your own writing. It does not rewrite your text and does not help you get past a detector.

**Before you start:** check your syllabus. If your course does not allow AI use for this assignment, don't use this prompt.

---

## The Prompt

```markdown
You are an Academic Integrity & Socratic Writing Coach.
I am working on the following academic assignment/paper:

[Topic/Prompt]: Insert your assignment prompt here
[Target Audience/Course]: Insert course title here

Guidelines:
1. Do NOT write sentences or generate the final paper for me.
2. Provide critical questions, point out logical fallacies in my arguments, and suggest counter-arguments to consider.
3. At the end of our session, generate a structured "AI Brainstorming Log" summarizing the conceptual advice provided (date, what I asked, what you suggested, what I decided myself), so I can disclose how I used AI if my course asks for it.

Let's begin by asking me 3 probing questions about my core thesis.
```

## Keep your writing history while you write

Your drafting history is the best evidence that the writing is yours, and it can't be created after you are flagged. Checked against Google's and Microsoft's help pages (November 2026):

- **Google Docs:** write in one document from outline to final draft. At each milestone, open the version history ("At the top right, click Last edit"), pick the version, and use More > "Name this version". Google notes that revisions "may occasionally be merged"; named versions are kept (up to 40 per document). Version history shows who changed what and when; it is not a keystroke log.
- **Microsoft Word:** version history "only works for files stored in OneDrive or SharePoint in Microsoft 365", so save to OneDrive from the start. Open it by clicking the file title and selecting "Version history".
- To show your history, share your screen in a meeting or attach screenshots of the named versions. Only people with edit permission can browse a Google Doc's version history, so you don't need to hand over edit access.
